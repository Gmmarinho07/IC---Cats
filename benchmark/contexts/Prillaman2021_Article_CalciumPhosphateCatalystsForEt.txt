========== ABSTRACT ==========

Abstract
The catalytic conversion of ethanol to butanol and butadiene at 633 K and atmospheric pressure (7 vol% ethanol) was 
studied over calcium phosphate materials pretreated over a wide temperature range. For calcium phosphate pretreated at 
or below 923 K, the resulting solid had an apatite structure identified by X-ray diffraction and strong acid sites probed by 
triethylamine (TEA) adsorption microcalorimetry. The low temperature pretreated materials were not effective at ethanol 
coupling reactions but instead were highly selective to acid-catalyzed products ethene and diethyl ether. Pretreatment of 
calcium phosphate at 973 K transformed the apatite structure to β-tricalcium phosphate, which exposed both acid and base 
sites evaluated by TEA and ­CO2 adsorption microcalorimetry. High temperature pretreated calcium phosphate catalysts 
formed the coupling products butanol and butadiene during ethanol reaction. The butadiene selectivity was improved by 
supporting calcium phosphate on zirconia.

To further explore the balance of surface and acid base 
sites necessary for producing butadiene from ethanol, CaP 
was mixed with ­SiO2 (a weakly acidic oxide) and with ­ZrO2 
(an amphoteric oxide). Table 3 presents the catalytic perfor-
mance of the individual ­SiO2 and ­ZrO2 catalysts in ethanol 
conversion, illustrating the very poor activity of silica (very 
low areal rate, near background conversion) and the high 
activity of zirconia for acid-catalyzed dehydration reactions. 
Given the amphoteric nature of ­ZrO2, it is not surprising 
that small amounts of C4 oxygenates and C4 olefins are 
produced from the ethanol reaction, but the selectivities to 
major products are 58% to light hydrocarbons and diethyl 
ether, and 33% to acetaldehyde, at an ethanol conversion of 
14%. Table 5 summarizes the catalytic performance of CaP/
SiO2 and CaP/ZrO2 in the conversion of ethanol at 633 K. 
Addition of ­SiO2 to CaP had little influence on the selectiv-
ity to products as the composite material produced mostly 
C4 oxygenates. In contrast, addition of ­ZrO2 to CaP cre-
ated a composite catalyst with suitable acid and base sites 
to produce butadiene with 19% selectivity at 10% ethanol 
conversion. Doubling the conversion to 22% also produced 
butadiene with 20% selectivity. The C4 oxygenate selectiv-
ity was low (< 5%) at both levels of conversion suggest-
ing that C4 coupling intermediates such as crotonaldehyde 
(see Scheme 1) that were formed on the composite cata-
lyst eventually transformed to butadiene. The acidity of the 
amphoteric ­ZrO2 component of the mixed catalyst was likely 
responsible for the high selectivity (57–63%) to light hydro-
carbons and diethyl ether. These results suggest that it may 
be possible to optimize the ratio of ­ZrO2 and CaP in the 
composite material to improve butadiene selectivity.

========== RESULTS ==========

Results and Discussion
Table 1 summarizes the nominal composition and meas-
ured surface area of the catalysts prepared and evaluated in 
this study. The surface area of the calcium phosphate (CaP) 
catalysts decreased monotonically with increasing treatment 
temperature (see Table 1), from 71 m2g−1 for an untreated 
sample to 5.4 m2g−1 after thermal treatment at 1173 K. For 
these calcium phosphate samples, thermal treatment induced 
a phase change from an apatite structure, present at room 
temperature through 923 K, to mostly β-Ca3(PO4)2 with rem-
nants of apatite at 973 K and higher temperatures. Figure 1 
presents the X-ray diffraction patterns of CaP before and 
after the various thermal treatments and clearly shows phase 
transition between 923 and 973 K.
