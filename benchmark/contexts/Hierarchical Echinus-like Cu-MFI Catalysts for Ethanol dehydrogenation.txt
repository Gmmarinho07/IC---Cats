Nevertheless, owing to the low Tamman temperature of Cu,
most Cu particles are prone to be deactivated because of the
aggregation of Cu in the presence of H2 byproduct at elevated
temperatures.20,21 Moreover, various Cu species, including
Cu0, Cu+, and Cu2+, are observed in Cu-based catalysts and are
indispensable during the dehydrogenation reaction. The
instability of Cu species in the presence of produced H2 also
leads to the challenge in identifying the role of Cu species in
ethanol conversion. Hence, it is highly desirable to develop an
eﬃcient catalyst with great stability in the non-oxidative
dehydrogenation of ethanol to acetaldehyde.
In this research, a hierarchical echinus-like catalyst of Cu-
MFI-AE (silicalite-1) was developed for the non-oxidative
dehydrogenation of ethanol to acetaldehyde. With this newly
developed catalyst, the selectivity to acetaldehyde reached 93%
under a conversion of 97% at 523 K and a WHSV (weight
hourly space velocity) of 0.53 h−1 in the 500 h TOS. This
unexpected activity of Cu-MFI-AE was attributed to the
synergistic eﬀect between Cu+ and Cu0 species on Cu-MFI-AE
catalysts, which activated the O−H and the nearby C−H
bonds, and promoted the H atoms migration and combination,
respectively. Meanwhile, the strong interfacial interactions

dehydrogenation of ethanol was proposed in Figure 4B.
Ethanol was preferably adsorbed on the Cu+ sites of catalysts,
which weakened the O−H bond and facilitated its cleavage.
Afterward, the absorbed ethanol was transformed to Cu(+)-
ethoxy and H atom, which were adsorbed on Cu+ and Cu0
sites, respectively. The C−H bond of Cu(+)-ethoxy was then
activated by Cu+ to produce the adsorbed acetaldehyde, and H
atom was transferred to Cu0 for combination.46 Finally, the
adsorbed hydrogen on Cu0 and the acetaldehyde on Cu+ were
desorbed to the gas phase. Diﬀerent from Cu−Cr based
catalysts,47 synergistic eﬀect of Cu+ and Cu0 species over the
Cu-MFI-AE catalyst ensured the ethanol dehydrogantion
reaction, rather than the estiﬁcation reaction.
