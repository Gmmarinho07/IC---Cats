========== ABSTRACT ==========

ABSTRACT: The catalytic upgrading of renewable ethanol to
C4−10 alcohols via C−C coupling offers a green and negative-
carbon-emission pathway toward value-added compounds. The
manipulation of catalysts’ surface basic and acidic properties is the
key to achieve high-selectivity C4−10 alcohols. In this study, we
present a solvent-free mechanochemical approach for the synthesis
of hydroxyapatite (HAP) catalysts with enhanced basicity. The
selectivity for a total C4−10 alcohols reaches 97.8% with a yield of
53.9% at 325 °C and 0.1 MPa, surpassing previously reported
catalysts in the literature. The mechanochemically synthesized
HAP catalysts extend along the c-axis and expose the (002) crystal
plane with enriched strong basic [Ca−O−P] sites. CO2-TPD and XPS analyses demonstrated that the hydrogen bonds between the
oxygen atoms of adjoining phosphate groups enhance the basic property of the catalyst surfaces. The kinetic measurements have
demonstrated that the abundance of strong basic sites facilitates the adsorption of ethanol molecules and accelerates the rate of C−C
coupling reactions, which is responsible for a high yield of C4−10 alcohols. This work offers a sustainable approach for synthesizing
such alcohols and stimulates the advancement of environmentally friendly catalysts.
KEYWORDS: ethanol, C−C coupling, mechanochemistry, hydroxyapatite, C4−10 alcohols

EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Catalyst Preparation. The mechanochemical synthesis was
carried out in a Fritsch Pulverisette 7 planetary ball mill with 80
mL tanks and a rotation speed of 800 rpm under an ambient
atmosphere. In all experiments, the weight ratio of the reactant blends
to milling beads was 1:20. The milling beads were zirconia balls with a
5 mm diameter and each mass of about 0.4 g. The duration of the ball
milling was 30 min. HAP can be obtained by mechanochemical
synthesis containing various mixtures including diverse calcium-
containing compounds, phosphates, etc., if the components of the
powder mixture are carefully selected based on the chemometric ratio
of Ca:P:O:H of the HAP (Scheme 1). The powder mixture was
formulated in a suitable molar ratio according to the following
chemical reactions 1−4, which were subsequently used to prepare
HAP, maintaining a theoretical Ca/P ratio of 1.67. The ball-milled
powder samples were calcined at 400 °C with a temperature ramp of 5
°C/min in air for 2 h. These samples are labeled HAP-1, HAP-2,
HAP-3, and HAP-4, respectively. The generation of 1 mol of HAP is
accompanied by the formation of different molar amounts of H2O in
each synthesis reaction. Commercial Ca3(PO4)2 calcined at 400 °C
were used as representative catalysts for comparison.

The type and number of acid/basic sites of the catalysts were analyzed

try (NH3/CO2-TPD-MS) on a Micromeritics AutoChem II 2920

Thermo Scientific K-α+ Spectrometer System using a monochromatic
Al Kα (1486.6 eV) X-ray source. Transmission electron microscopy

instrument operated at 300 kV. Fourier transform infrared spectros-
copy (FT-IR) spectra were obtained on a Nicolet 6700 spectrometer.
In situ ethanol-diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform (DRIFT)
spectra were captured using a Bruker 70 V spectrometer with a
reaction cell (HARRICK) and MCT detector. The time-dependent
adsorption profiles of ethanol were recorded on an IGA-100
Intelligent Gravimetric Analyzer (Hiden analytical).

or CO2 that might have been adsorbed on the catalyst surface. The

The adsorption of ethanol on the HAP surface constitutes
the first step in the coupling reaction. To investigate the
diffusion behavior of ethanol in HAP, the time-resolved gas
adsorption of ethanol on HAP was measured (Figures 3c and
S10, S11). HAP-3-0.44, the more basic sites catalyst, exhibited
higher ethanol gas uptake, approximately 1.2 times higher than
that of the HAP-3 catalyst. Interestingly, the ethanol diffusional
time constant (D′= D/r2, s−1) for HAP-3-0.44 catalyst is 5.04
× 10−4, which is 1.6 times higher than that of 3.14 × 10−4 for
HAP-3 catalyst. As we presumed, the Lewis strong basic sites
on the catalyst surface facilitate the adsorption of ethanol
molecules for the subsequent C−C coupling reaction.
