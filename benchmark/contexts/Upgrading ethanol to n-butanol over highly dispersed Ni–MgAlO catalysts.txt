Hydrotalcite is a good precursor for preparing Ni–MgAlO cata-
lysts with desired dispersions of Ni, acid, and base sites and gives
Ni particles of size ca. 5 nm. The presence of Ni facilitated hydrogen

hydrogenation, because of its special hydrogen adsorption and des-
orption properties. The composite oxides had relatively strong base
and moderate acid pairs for aldol condensation reactions. The
numbers of metal, acid, and base sites were well balanced on the
Ni4MgAlO catalyst, which showed the best selectivity for n-
butanol, with enhanced ethanol conversion. Finally, a reaction net-
work for ethanol conversion to different products is proposed.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Catalytic conversion of ethanol
3.1.1. Ethanol conversion over various catalysts
The Mg/Al ratio in oxide catalysts greatly affects the n-butanol
selectivity and ethanol conversion [34]; therefore the Mg/Al ratio
in the Ni/MgAlO catalysts was ﬁrst optimized. As shown in Fig. 1,
the ethanol conversion over the 5%Ni/Al2O3 catalyst was 25%, with
35% selectivity for n-butanol. Large amounts of diethyl ether and
ethylene were produced; this is attributed to dehydration reac-
tions occurring at strong acid sites on the catalyst [6,35]. To lower
the density of strong acid sites, MgO was introduced into the car-
rier, using a coprecipitation method. The ethanol conversion
decreased with increasing Mg/Al ratio, but the selectivity for
high-carbon alcohols increased. At an Mg/Al ratio of 4:1, the selec-
tivity for n-butanol increased to 50.2%, and the selectivity for C4–C8
alcohols reached 77.3%. The Mg/Al ratio was therefore set at 4:1 for
catalyst evaluation to obtain high yields of primary alcohols.
The effects of the Ni content on alcohol selectivity and ethanol
conversion were investigated. As shown in Fig. 2, over the mixed
oxide catalyst in the absence of Ni, the predominant product was
n-butanol, with a selectivity of 58%, but the ethanol conversion
was 0.5%. Increasing the Ni content from zero to 19.5 wt.% led to
a monotonic increase in ethanol conversion from 0.5% to 18.8%,
without a decrease in the selectivity for n-butanol. Over the
optimal Ni4MgAlO (19.5 wt.% Ni) catalyst, the selectivities for
n-butanol and total C4–C8 alcohols reached 55.2% and 85%, respec-
tively (ratios of C4:C6:C8 were 8:3:1). However, further increasing
the Ni content to 45.2 wt.% signiﬁcantly decreased the alcohol
selectivity. A large amount of gaseous byproducts was produced,
including hydrogen, carbon monoxide, methane, and carbon
dioxide; these products acc
