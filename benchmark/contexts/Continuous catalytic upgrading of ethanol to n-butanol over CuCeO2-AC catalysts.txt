summary, highly efficient Cu-CeO2/AC catalysts prepared 
by a wetness impregnation method were used for continuous 
catalytic upgrading of ethanol to n-butanol. The Cu-CeO2/AC 
catalysts exhibited average 40% of selectivity towards n-butanol 
and nearly 20% of n-butanol yield at lower temperature (523 K) 
and pressure (2 MPa N2). The high dispersion of Cu metals is 
favorable for high ethanol conversion, while the presence of 
highly dispersive Ce species as basic sites is beneficial for the 
aldol condensation of acetaldehyde, which drives the reaction 
equilibrium towards n-butanol production. Additionally, the 
stability of Cu-CeO2/AC catalysts can be improved due to the 
interaction between Cu metals and these Ce species. The yield 
of n-butanol is also influenced by the nature of the support, so 
that higher ethyl acetate yield and subsequent lower n-butanol 
yield are observed over catalysts containing SiO2 or Al2O3. 
Therefore, the high catalytic activity, selectivity towards n-
butanol and n-butanol yield could be ascribed to the synergy of 
Cu, CeO2 and the activated carbon support in upgrading ethanol 
to n-butanol.

Upgrading of bio-ethanol to n-butanol via homogeneous or 
heterogeneous catalytic reactions has currently received great 
attention both in the scientific and industrial fields, in view of 
the advantages of n-butanol over bio-ethanol as a sustainable 
fuel. As more preferable catalysts in industry, heterogeneous 
catalysts have been comprehensively investigated for n-butanol 
production from ethanol in last decade. A range of basic 
zeolites,1 MgO,2 Mg-Al metal oxides3 and hydroxyapaties (HAP)4 
catalyst systems were firstly employed for this reaction. 
Although high reaction temperature (≥573 K) and pressure (≥6.0 
MPa) were used, the yield of n-butanol was normally lower than 
10%. Incorporating metals in catalysts could significantly 
improve the conversion of ethanol, as a result of the enhanced 
dehydrogenation of ethanol on metals. For example, Ni/Al2O3 
catalyst exhibited up to 80% of selectivity to n-butanol at 25% 
ethanol conversion after reaction of 72 h at 523 K and 7 MPa 
(Ar) in a batch reactor.5 However, due to the strong cracking 
capability of C-C bonds of metal Ni, considerable amount of 
gaseous carbon products such as CO, CO2, CH4, were also 
detected. Similar catalytic performance was reported in a fixed-
bed reactor over basic aluminum oxide supported Cu, Ni and Co 
catalysts for continuous catalytic upgrading of ethanol to n-
butanol at 513 K and 7 MPa.6 More recently, Poliakoff and his 
co-workers have exploited a high surface area CeO2 (HSACeO2) 
supported Cu catalyst for upgrading ethanol to n-butanol in the 
presence of supercritical CO2 in a fixed-bed reactor,7 which 
exhibited 45% selectivity to n-butanol and up to 30% yield of n-
butanol. Though the yield of n-butanol is the highest ever 
reported on heterogeneous catalysts, the use of supercritical

Active carbon (AC) is widely used as catalyst itself or support 
for precious metals such as Pd, Pt and Ru,8 due to its unique 
properties such as extremely high specific surface area, 
controllable porosity and surface chemistry, and high stability 
at various pH values. On the other hand, Cu-based catalysts, 
especially Al2O3 and SiO2 supported Cu catalysts are well known 
as very active heterogeneous catalysts for various reactions, 
such as steam reforming of methanol, CO oxidation, 
hydrogenation and dehydrogenation reactions.9 However, AC 
supported copper catalysts are less applied in catalytic reactions, 
because of the liable sintering of Cu nanoparticles engendered 
by the weak metal-support interaction.10 In the present work, 
we first reported that Cu-CeO2/AC catalysts prepared by a 
simple impregnation method demonstrated superior catalytic 
performance for ethanol upgrading to n-butanol.
