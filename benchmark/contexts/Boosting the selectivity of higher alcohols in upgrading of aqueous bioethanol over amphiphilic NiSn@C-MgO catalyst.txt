========== EXPERIMENTAL ==========

Experimental section 
2.1. Catalyst preparation 
2.1.1. Preparation of NiSn@C-MgO 
NiSn@C-MgO catalysts were prepared by a facile sol–gel method 
followed by pyrolysis and carbonization strategy. 10 mmol Ni 
(NO3)2⋅6H2O, 20 mmol Mg(NO3)2⋅6H2O, 20 mmol C6H8O7⋅H2O and 0.5 
mmol SnCl4⋅5H2O were successively dissolved in 60 mL deionized water 
under continuous stirring to obtain a homogeneous solution. Subse­
quently, the obtained mixture was gradually gelled by evaporation at 
100 ◦C and then dried at 80 ◦C for 48 h in an oven. Finally, the resultant 
precursor was calcined in a N2 flow of 20 mL/min at 550 ◦C for 3 h, with 
the yielded catalyst labeled as NiSn@C-MgO-550 (abbreviated as 
NiSn@C-MgO). The catalysts with carbonization temperature of 400 ◦C, 
500 ◦C, 600 ◦C and 700 ◦C were denoted as NiSn@C-MgO-400, NiSn@C- 
MgO-500, NiSn@C-MgO-600 and NiSn@C-MgO-700, respectively. 
2.1.2. Preparation of NiSn/MgO 
NiSn/MgO was synthesized through the pyrolysis method followed 
by hydrogen reduction. Firstly, 10 mmol Ni(NO3)2⋅6H2O, 20 mmol Mg 
(NO3)2⋅6H2O and 0.5 mmol SnCl4⋅5H2O were successively dissolved in 
deionized water (60 mL) under continuous stirring to obtain a homo­
geneous solution at room temperature. After vigorously stirring for 2 h, 
the mixture was dried at 80 ◦C for 24 h in an oven. Finally, the resultant 
precursor was reduced at 550 ◦C under 20 mL/min H2 flow for 3 h. 
2.1.3. Preparation of NiSn@C 
NiSn@C was synthesized by sol–gel method and one-step pyrolysis/ 
carbonization strategy. Firstly, 10 mmol Ni(NO3)2⋅6H2O, 20 mmol 
C6H8O7⋅H2O and 0.5 mmol SnCl4⋅5H2O were successively dissolved in 
60 mL deionized water under continuous stirring to obtain a homoge­
neous solution. Subsequently, the obtained mixture was gradually gelled 
by evaporation at 100 ◦C and then dried at 80 ◦C for 48 h in an oven. 
Finally, the resultant precursor was calcined at 550 ◦C under 20 mL/min 
N2 flow for 3 h.

========== RESULTS ==========

Results and discussion 
3.1. Catalyst characterization 
NiSn@C-MgO, synthesized via a facile sol–gel method followed by 
pyrolysis and carbonization strategy, displays amphipathy due to the co- 
existence of carbon shell (lipophilic) and MgO (hydrophilic) (Fig. 1). 
Specifically, citric acid was used as reductant, surface ligand, as well as 
the carbon shell precursor, while MgO was used as the hydrophilic 
support. Conventionally, the structure, thickness and morphology of 
carbon shell are greatly affected by carbonization temperature, which 
has a significant impact on catalyst performance, especially for amphi­
philic catalysts [32–34]. X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement was 
performed to study the composition of the material and the structure or 
morphology of the atoms or molecules. Fig. 2a shows the X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) of the NiSn@C-MgO catalysts prepared at different 
carbonization temperatures. The peaks at 42.8◦, 62.2◦and 78.5◦in the 
XRD patterns are ascribed to the (200), (220) and (222) facets of MgO 
(JCPDS No. 74-1225). Moreover, the peaks at 44.5◦, 51.8◦and 76.4◦are 
corresponded to Ni (111), (200) and (220) facets, respectively (JCPDS 
No. 70–1849). The XRD diffraction peaks of Ni and MgO with lower 
peak intensities are observed at the carbonization temperature of 
400 ◦C. With the increase of carbonization temperature, the diffraction
