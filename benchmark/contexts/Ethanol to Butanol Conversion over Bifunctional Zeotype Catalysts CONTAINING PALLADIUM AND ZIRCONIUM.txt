in the Guerbet process.

Catalytic Testing Procedure
Ethanol conversion was studied in a flow reactor at

fed for 1 h to reduce palladium particles; after that, the
temperature was set at a required level. The tests were
conducted at a constant stream of nitrogen or hydro-

Surface Compounds According to IR Data
The structure and concentration of surface com-
pounds formed in the reaction of ethanol and a
Pd/Zr–BEA catalyst were studied by IR spectroscopy.
The sample was saturated with ethanol vapors at 25°C
and then heated to 250°C with a stepwise increase in
temperature; IR spectra of the surface compounds
were recorded after cooling to 25°C (Fig. 5).
During ethanol adsorption, a “negative” band at
3732 cm–1 appears in the spectra owing to the forma-
tion of a hydrogen bond between the OH group of eth-
anol and the Si-OH…O(H)-C2H5 silanol group. The
broad band at ~3400 cm–1 corresponds to the same
complex. In the region of the C–H stretching vibra-
tions, the spectrum exhibits bands at 2980 (νas(CH3)),
2937 (νas(CH2)), 2904 (νs(CH3)), and 2880 cm–1
(νs(CH2)), which are attributed to the ethyl group of
ethanol molecules [17]. In the region of bending vibra-
tions, bands characteristic of ethanol are located at
1483 (δas(CH2)), 1451 (δas(CH3)), and 1395 cm–1
(δs(CH). Upon heating to 50°C, the spectra exhibit a
band of the carbonyl group vibrations ν(C=O) at
1724 cm–1 and less intense broad bands at 1686 and
1641 cm–1. This finding indicates the occurrence of
dehydrogenation reactions to 

composition to car-
bonates. It is significant that, under real catalysis con-
ditions, metal sites can be blocked by substantially
sorbed acetates and carbonates. Therefore, ethanol
can mostly react on acid sites to form diethyl ether.

Effect of Hydrogen on Ethanol Conversion
In terms of the Guerbet reaction stoichiometry, the
addition of hydrogen is not necessary, because hydro-
gen is evolved during in situ ethanol dehydrogenation.
In this case, hydrogen from the gas phase can reduce
the surface acetates and carbonates, which are readily
formed from ethanol; this reduction can contribute to
the purification of the Pd surface. On the other hand,
hydrogen additionally fed to the system can undergo
butanol hydrogenation to butane via the intermediate
formation of butenes. To verify these assumptions,
comparative tests with and without hydrogen feeding
were conducted (Table 1).
In the case of reaction without the addition of
hydrogen, the main products formed over the catalyst
are acetaldehyde, diethyl ether, and butenes. Appar-
ently, ethanol dehydrogenation occurs quite rapidly;
afterwards, the resulting acetaldehyde slowly under-
