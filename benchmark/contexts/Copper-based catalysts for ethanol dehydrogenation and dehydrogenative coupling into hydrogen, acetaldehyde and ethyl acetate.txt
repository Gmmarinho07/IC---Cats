Effect of particle size and dispersion Cu particles
The particle size and dispersion of Cu particles are the
important factors affecting the catalytic conversion and
product
selectivity
of
ethanol
dehydrogenation.
Fig.
5a
showed that the small particle size gives high ethanol con-
version using Cu/silicon carbide support [61]. The same
manner is also observed over different catalysts, Cu/SiO2, Cu/
PPC and Cu/silicon carbide, with high ethanol conversion
>80% at Cu particle size <10 nm (Fig. 5c) [61,76,80,81]. That can
be explained that small Cu particles possess a higher density
of coordinatively unsaturated sites, e.g. corners and kinks,
which are more reactive than steps or terrace sites [76,79].
However, at low Cu particle size (<10 nm), the ethanol con-
version is also low (<40%) over some catalysts due to the effect
of support to Cu-based catalysts. Indeed, as the same small Cu
particle size (<10 nm), the mesoporous support (61% ethanol
conversion over Cu/MgAlOx-P) shows high catalytic activity
than that of non-porous support (28% ethanol conversion over
Cu/MgAlOx) [82]. Moreover, SiO2 support exhibits higher
ethanol conversion (85.4% ethanol conversion) than that of
carbon support (17.4% ethanol conversion) [80]. Looking at the
dispersion of Cu particles, the dispersion of Cu particles in the
range of 20%e60% gives high ethanol conversion (Fig. 5d).
Generally, the high Cu dispersion will provide a high amount
of active sites resulting in high ethanol conversion. But with
low Cu loading leads to low total Cu active sites and low
ethanol conversion. In Fig. 5a, the high Cu dispersion showed
high ethanol conversion (81.4%) in the case of Cu/SiO2/SiC
catalyst. This trend is relative to the high active sites resulting
from the high dispersion of Cu species

The different trends in particle size and dispersion can be
presented by TOF over Cu/0.675AleZr and Cu/Zr catalyst

increase of particle size and a decrease of Cu dispersion,
whereas that trend is reverse using Cu/0.675AleZr catalyst
[72]. That indicates that the interface dominates the catalyst
activity rather than the Cu active sites [72].
For the product selectivity, small particle size usually favors
acetaldehyde formation, while larger copper particles can
favor ethyl acetate production [82]. Therefore, the particle size
of Cu particles can be adjusted to select the desired products.
In 2020, Zhang et al. [76] showed a promising Cu/SiO2
catalyst with >90% ethanol conversion and >95% acetalde-
hyde selectivity. Cu/SiO2 with 10 wt% Cu showed well
dispersion of Cu particles (38.7%) and small particle size
(2.7 nm) using the ammonium evaporation method. The
HRTEM image showed that no CuO particles were observed
even at 5 nm resolution at 600 C calcination (Fig. 6). Sug-
gesting the ammonia evaporation method is an excellent
method to prepare a high dispersion of Cu on the support.

Support
As discussed in the effect of Cu particles, the support can
provide a positive or negative effect on the catalyst. In fact, the
interface of the Cu-support is very important in tailoring the
products because different support can direct different reac-
tion pathways and the catalyst activity.
The support with high acidity favors ethanol dehydration
into ethylene, while ethanol dehydrogenation takes place on
weak acid or basis sites [76,83,84]. Phung et al. [85] reported
that catalysts possessing both acid and base sites (MgOeAl2-
O3) showed high acetaldehyde selectivity than that of ZrO2,
SiO2, and TiO2. While the strong Lewis acid g-Al2O3 did not
observe acetaldehyde and mainly ethanol dehydration prod-
ucts. In addition, Zhang et al. [76] reported that Cu/SiO2
showed the best ethanol conversion (X > 90%) and acetalde-
hyde selectivity (S > 98%) at 240 C in comparison to Cu/ZrO2
(X ¼ 52%, S ¼ 86%), Cu/La2O3 (X ¼ 43%, S ¼ 98.5%), Cu/MgO
(X ¼ 60%, S ¼ 98.5%), and Cu/MSU-9 (X ¼ 78%, S ¼ 52%;
MSU ¼ mesoporous silica-alumina Si/Al ¼ 9).

The support can enhance the ethanol conversion and
ethanol dehydrogenation reaction to form hydrogen, acetal-
dehyde, and further reaction to ethyl acetate. Good support
improves the dispersion of Cu on the surface of the catalyst
leading to an increase in acetaldehyde selectivity and stability
[86]. Indeed, hybrid C@SiO2 hollow submicron spheres showed
a high dispersion of Cu species due to the highly dispersed
silica species among carbons [86]. The Cu/C@SiO2 performed
36.1% ethanol conversion and ~99% acetaldehyde selectivity
at 260 C. Additionally, the high dispersion of Cu on the sur-
face of the hybrid carbon-silica prevents the sintering and
agglomeration of copper particles during the reaction process
resulting in the improvement of the catalyst stability.
Not only the high dispersion of Cu species on the support
but also the interaction of Cu species with support and the
interaction of intermediate and product with support are
important [61]. For example, ethyl acetate is formed a high
yield on the surface of Cu/ZrO2, but not on the surface of Cu/
SiO2 þ ZrO2 [87], conﬁrming the role of the interface Cu-
support on the dehydrogenative coupling reaction. Recently,
Finger et al. [38] performed the effect of metal oxides on Cu-
based catalysts, they found that the rate of ethyl acetate in-
creases with the addition of metal oxides with the trend: no-
metal oxides adding < Cr2O3 < Al2O3- < ZrO2 ¼ ZnO. The dif-
ference in ethyl acetate rate is due to the effect of the interface
Cu-support. In fact, the metal oxides support can tailor the
agglomeration of Cu nanoparticles showing different Cu
dispersion [38]. Also, the addition of metal oxides can also
