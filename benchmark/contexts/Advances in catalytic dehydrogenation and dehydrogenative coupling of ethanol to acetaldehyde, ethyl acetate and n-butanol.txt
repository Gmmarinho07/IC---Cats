For example, Wang et al. [49] demonstrated the superior performance of

In addition, doping various metal promoters into Cu-based catalysts 
to form bimetallic catalysts can effectively regulate product selectivity, 
alter reaction pathways, and significantly enhance catalyst stability. 
Samira Amokrane et al. [26] added transition metals (Ag, Co, Ni, Cd and 
Pt) to copper to investigate the ethanol dehydrogenation process on a 
copper bimetallic systems. Monometallic copper has been shown to 
agglomerate and deactivate, which can be prevented by adding Ag, Co, 
Cd, or Ni. Among them, Ni–Cu improves conversion but reduces acet­
aldehyde selectivity by producing the byproduct CH4. Ag reacts weakly 
with ethanol, resulting in decreased conversion. Pt changes the chemical 
route, causing the process to yield diethyl ether. It is worth noting that 
Cd–Cu greatly increases the sintering resistance of the catalysts, 
increasing their stability.

Wu et al. [91] prepared M-CeO2/ activated carbon (AC) (M = Cu, Fe, 
Co, Ni, Pd) catalysts via the wetness impregnation method and evalu­
ated their performance in the catalytic upgrading of ethanol to n-butanol 
under mild conditions (250 ◦C, 2 MPa, N2/ethanol (v/v) = 500:1). The 
results showed that the Pd–CeO2/AC catalyst exhibited the highest n- 
butanol selectivity (67.6 %), while the Cu–CeO2/AC catalyst achieved 
the highest ethanol conversion (46.2 %) with moderate n-butanol 
selectivity (41.3 %). This was attributed to the strong hydrogen acti­
vation and spillover ability of Pd metal, which significantly promoted 
the CeO2 reduction and the formation of surface basic sites to enhance 
the crotonaldehyde hydrogenation efficiency. In contrast, Cu metal 
dominated ethanol conversion due to its superior dehydrogenation 
capability.
Furthermore, the Pd@UiO-66 catalyst [89] (2 wt% Pd) discussed in 
the previous section exhibits excellent performance in the conversion of 
ethanol to n-butanol. Under conditions of 250 ◦C and 2 MPa, it achieves 
an ethanol conversion rate of 49.8 % and an n-butanol selectivity of 
48.6 %, while maintaining stability over a long reaction time of 200 h. 
This is attributed to the high activity of the palladium active sites in the 
steps of ethanol dehydrogenation and crotonaldehyde hydrogenation, as 
well as the Lewis acidic Zr6 nodes, which promote the aldol condensa­
tion of acetaldehyde.

4.3. Non-noble metal catalysts
Traditional noble metal catalysts exhibit excellent activity in the 
ethanol dehydrogenative coupling to n-butanol, but their high cost and 
resource scarcity limit industrial applications. In contrast, non-noble 
metal catalysts have attracted attention due to their low cost, abun­
dant resources, and structural tunability.
Zhou et al. [94] precisely engineered Cu@UiO-66 catalysts, suc­
cessfully revealing the critical role of spatial proximity between active 
sites in the tandem catalytic upgrading of ethanol to n-butanol. The 
study identified that Cu nanoparticles confined within the pores of UiO- 
66 and the Lewis acid-oxygen vacancy pairs (Zr3–□) on Zr nodes serve 
as the genuine active centers. The close contact between them efficiently 
synergizes to catalyze three key steps: ethanol dehydrogenation, acet­

3.2. Pd-based catalysts
Studies have shown that supported Pd catalysts achieve high activity 
and selectivity through unique electronic structure modulation and in­
termediate stabilization mechanisms [81–84].
On monometallic Pd catalysts, Pd0 not only acts as the active site for 
ethanol dehydrogenation but also facilitates the further decomposition 
of acetaldehyde into C1 products (such as CH4 and CO), thereby 
reducing the selectivity for the target product. Therefore, inhibiting 
ethanol decomposition is crucial for the selective conversion of ethanol 
to ethyl acetate. Iwasa et al. [81] demonstrated that palladium-based 
catalysts, by forming alloys with metals such as zinc, gallium, and in­
dium (Pd–Zn, Pd–Ga, and Pd–In), can inhibit the ethanol decompositi

3.3. Other types of catalysts
Despite the excellent catalytic activity and stability of CuCr-based 
catalysts, the toxicity of Cr and the low stability of Cu particles limit 
their practical applications. Moreover, Cu-based catalysts frequently 
undergo side reactions, such as C–C bond cleavage and condensation 
that lead to product complexity and catalyst deactivation.
For this purpose, Yin et al. [85] chemically tailored Nickel-based 
catalyst (Ni-based) catalysts by introducing In, and successfully devel­
oped the Ni0.1Zn0.7Al0.3InOx catalyst for the dehydrogenative coupling 
of ethanol into ethyl acetate. Under conditions of 275 ◦C and a weight 
hourly space velocity (WHSV) of 1.9 h−1, the catalyst achieved 46.2 % 
ethanol conversion and 90.1 % ethyl acetate selectivity, while main­
taining stable performance during a 370-hour time on stream. It was 
shown that the introduction of In led to the formation of Ni4In alloy 
active sites, which effectively inhibited the C–C bond cleavage and 
condensation side reactions during ethanol conversion by modulating 
the electronic properties of Ni. DFT calculations further confirmed that 
the energy barrier for acetaldehyde dehydrogenation to acetyl species 
on the Ni4In(110) crystal surface was 0.41 eV, significantly lower than 
that of the 1.89 eV on the pure Ni(111) surface, elucidating the efficient 
selective regulation mechanism of alloy sites in dehydrogenation 
reactions.

method for the gas-phase conversion of ethanol to ethyl acetate and 
other products. The introduction of Cu and Ni significantly enhanced the 
dehydrogenation activity and promoted ethanol conversion to ethyl
