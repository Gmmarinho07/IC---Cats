improved overall performance in the methanol conversion process.
Methods
Catalyst preparation and characterization
Proton-type ZSM-5 zeolites (Si/Al = 11.5, Zeolyst) were utilized as cat-
alysts for all the reactions unless otherwise indicated. A control ZSM-5
sample enriched with Lewis acid sites was prepared by calcination in
air at 973 K for 6 h. The sample was packed into a shallow bed con-
ﬁguration on a quartz crucible. The temperature was increased gra-
dually from room temperature to the target temperature at a rate of
1 °C/min. The structure and crystalline nature of ZSM-5 zeolites were
examined by a PANAlytical X’Pert Powder X-ray diffraction dif-
fractometer with a Cu Kα radiation using a step of 0.02° at a respective
voltage of 40 kV and a current of 40 mA. The scanning range was 5°
to 40°.
The acid concentration of catalysts was determined by IR of pyr-
idine adsorption experiments. The measurements were performed on
a Bruker Tensor 27 spectromete

. The efﬂuent products at the moment of quenching
were analyzed using GC-MS to establish a correlation between the
efﬂuent products and the surface-adsorbed species on zeolites.
The efﬂuent products were analyzed by GC-MS (Shimadzu GCMS
QP-2010) equipped with RT-Q-BOND capillary column (30 m, 0.32 mm
i.d., 10.0 μm ﬁlm thickness) or Petrocol DH capillary column (100 m,
0.25 mm i.d., 0.5 μm ﬁlm thickness). The temperature programming of
RT-Q-BOND capillary column started at 40 °C (maintained for 1 min),
followed by a rate of 5 °C per min to a 140 °C and a rate of 10 °C per min
to a ﬁnal temperature of 230 °C (maintained for 5 min). The tempera-
ture programming of Petrocol DH capillary column started at 40 °C
(maintained for 2 min), followed by a rate of 1 °C per min to a 50 °C
(maintained for 5 min) and a rate of 15 °C per min to a ﬁnal temperature
of 230 °C. When checking the efﬂuent products, the line from the
reactor outlet to the GC was maintained at 100 °C to avoid any con-
densation of heavy hydrocarbons. The retained species were also
analyzed by GC MS (Shimadzu GCMS QP-2010) equipped with Petrocol
DH capillary column (100 m, 0.25 mm i.d., 0.5 μm ﬁlm thickness). The
following temperature programming was applied: an initial tempera-
ture of 40 °C was maintained for 20 min, followed by a rate of 10 °C per
min to 220 °C (maintained for 15 min) and a rate of 15 °C per min to
250 °C (maintained for 5 min).

Results
BAS catalyzed MPV reduction over ZSM-5 zeolite
Proton-type ZSM-5 zeolites (Si/Al = 11.5) were utilized as catalysts to
investigate the behavior of acetaldehyde during the methanol con-
version. Detailed structural and acidity analyses are provided in
Table S1 and Figs. S1–S3. The 27Al MAS NMR spectra indicate the pre-
sence of only tetra-coordinated Al species at 55 ppm in the ZSM-5
zeolite (Fig. S2). The concentrations of BAS (590 μmol·g−1) and Lewis
acid sites (LAS) (6 μmol·g−1) were determined using IR spectroscopy of
adsorbed pyridine. The high concentration of BAS and negligible LAS
suggest that the methanol reaction would be predominantly catalyzed
by the BAS on ZSM-5 (Table S1). Methanol-only (Fig. S4) and methanol-
acetaldehyde (vmethanol/vacetaldehyde = 4:1) co-feeding (Fig. 2) experi-
ments were conducted over ZSM-5 at elevated temperatures of 373,
423, 473, 523, and 573 K. To minimize secondary reactions and capture
intermediates, the reactions were performed for a short duration of 8 s
using a pulse-quench reactor, where the catalytic bed was rapidly
quenched with liquid nitrogen within 0.5 s. The reactor efﬂuent pro-
ducts were analyzed using connected gas chromatography (GC).
Methanol alone did not yield hydrocarbons, aside from dimethyl
ether (CH3OCH3), attemperatures below 573 K (Fig. S4), indicating that
an active HP mechanism is absent under these conditions. However,
introducing acetaldehyde signiﬁcantly impacts methanol conversion
over the zeolites. As shown in Fig. 2a, substantial amounts of methyl

result demonstrates that BAS can also catalyze the MPV reaction in
methanol conversion.
As the temperature rises to 423 K, methyl formate yield increases,
reﬂecting enhanced methanol oxidation. Ethanol production also
becomes evident. Further dehydration of ethanol with methanol leads
to ethyl methyl ether (CH3CH2OCH3) and ethene. Notably, ethene
dominates the hydrocarbon products with an ethylene to propylene
(E/P) ratio of 5.02 at 473 K, suggesting the MPV pathway as the primary
route for hydrocarbon production. This ethene selectivity persists at
higher temperatures, indicating continued dominance of the MPV
mechanism. However, propene and heavier hydrocarbons also
increase with temperature, leading to a declining E/P ratio (from 5.02
to 1.22). This suggests the concurrent participation of the HP

The observed MPV reduction suggests that acetaldehyde formed
during methanol conversion reacts with unreacted methanol to produce
ethene as the primary product. We hypothesize that during the initial
stages of methanol conversion, when methanol is abundant, acet-
aldehyde preferentially interacts with surrounding methanol molecules,
favoring the MPV reaction. To verify this, we conducted co-feeding
experiments with a low acetaldehyde concentration (v/v, 1:100). As
shown in FigureS7, ethene isvirtuallythe solealkene product,conﬁrming
the dominance of the MPV reaction under high methanol environments,
resembling the early stages of methanol conversion.

Relationship between surface species and efﬂuent products
during the MPV reduction
The time-dependent transformation of acetaldehyde during methanol
conversion over ZSM-5 at 523 K was investigated using ¹³C NMR
experiments to analyze surface species and GC to analyze efﬂuent
products (Fig. 4). As shown in Fig. 4a and Fig. S11, the ¹³C NMR spec-
trum reveals the presence of SES even after a very short reaction time
of 8 s. This indicates the immediate operation of the MPV reaction
during the initial stages (Fig. 4c). Consequently, ethene dominates the
efﬂuent products at this point, with an E/P ratio of 2.82 (Fig. 4b). This
dominance can be attributed to the decomposition of SES formed
through the MPV reaction. Although some aromatics are also detected
by ¹³C NMR at this early stage, their contribution to the product
stream seems limited, as evidenced by the minimal formation of C3+
hydrocarbons in the GC analysis. However, as the reaction progresses
beyond 16 s, the ¹³C NMR signal for SES rapidly diminishes, suggesting
a weakening of the MPV reaction (Fig. 4a and Fig. S11). Conversely, the
HP route appears to take precedence, although its overall reactivity
remains limited at 523 K (Fig. 4c). This shift in reaction pathways is
reﬂected in the product distribution (Fig. 4b). The selectivity of C3+
products, particularly propene, increases, leading to a decrease in the
E/P ratio below 1. This observed trend aligns with typical methanol
reaction over zeolites, where ethene initially dominates due to a more
