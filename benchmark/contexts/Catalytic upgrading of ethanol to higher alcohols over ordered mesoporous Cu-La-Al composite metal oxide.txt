========== EXPERIMENTAL ==========

Experimental 
2.1. Catalyst preparation 
All chemicals used in this procedure were AR grade. A series of 
ordered mesoporous Cu-La-Al composite metal oxides were prepared by 
EISA method as follows: 2 g of P123 (EO20PO70EO20) was firstly dis­
solved in 20 mL anhydrous ethanol. Then, 0.286 g salicylic acid, 0.2 g 
citric acid and calculated amounts of La(Ac)3⋅xH2O and Cu(Ac)2 were 
added into above solution. With complete dissolution of all solutes, a 
clear solution denoted solution A was achieved. Solution B was achieved 
by adding 3.2 mL 35 wt% HCl and subsequently 4.08 g (20 mmol) 
aluminum isopropoxide into another 20 mL anhydrous ethanol. After 
the solution A and B were mixed and stirred vigorously for 5 h at room 
temperature, the ethanol solvent was evaporated at 333 K for 72 h. The 
resulting solid samples were heated at a rate of 1 K/min to 673 K for 4 h, 
and then calcined at 973 K for 1 h with a heating rate of 10 K/min. The 
ordered mesoporous Cu-La-Al composite metal oxides obtained finally 
were denoted as OM-CuxLayAl100, where the subscripts indicate the 
relative molar number (RMN) of Cu, La and Al, respectively. As refer­
ence, ordered mesoporous Al2O3, La-Al and Cu-Al composite metal ox­
ides (denoted as OMA, OM-CuxAl100, and OM-LayAl100, respectively) 
were prepared in the same process without the addition of lanthanum 
acetate and/or copper acetate. 
Stoichiometric copper aluminate spinel (CuAl2O4) catalyst was pre­
pared by grinding Al(NO3)3⋅9H2O and Cu(NO3)2⋅3H2O with Al/Cu 
molar ratio of 2:1 to uniform powder mixture in quartz inkstone. The 
powder mixture was then calcined at 1073 K for 6 h with a heat ramp of 
10 K/min and then the CuAl2O4 sample was obtained.

H2 temperature-programmed reduction (H2-TPR) were performed on

conductivity detector (TCD) and a OmniStar GSD320 mass spectrometer 
(MS). Typically, the sample (100 mg) was pretreated in a quartz U-tube 
reactor with a He gas stream (30 ml/min) at 473 K for 1 h. After cooling 
to room temperature, a 10% H2/Ar flow (50 ml/min) was introduced 
and the sample was heated to 1273 K (10 K/min) while desorbed species 
were monitored by the thermal conductivity detector (TCD) and mass 
spectrometry (MS) in real time. 
CO2 temperature-programmed desorption (CO2-TPD) and NH3 tem­
perature -programmed desorption (NH3-TPD) were also performed on 
the Micromeritics Autochem II 2920 chemisorber. For CO2-TPD exper­
iments, the sample (100 mg) was pretreated in a quartz U-tube reactor 
with a He gas stream (30 mL/min) at 473 K for 1 h. When the temper­
ature decreased to 323 K, CO2 saturation uptake of the pretreated cat­
alysts was achieved by passing pure CO2 for 0.5 h at 30 mL/min. After 
the CO2 adsorption, the catalyst was purged by Ar flow (40 mL/min) for 
1 h at 323 K. Then the temperature was linearly increased from 323 K to 
1073 K (10 K/min) in Ar flow while desorbed species were monitored by 
the thermal conductivity detector (TCD) and mass spectrometry (MS) in 
real time.⋅NH3-TPD experiments were carried out by similar procedure 
as that of CO2-TPD experiments except for using 10% NH3/Ar gas 
mixture as the adsorbate.

Small-angle XRD patterns of this series of catalysts before and after

3.7. Catalytic mechanism 
The product distribution over OM-CuxLayAl100 catalysts indicated 
that higher alcohols were produced from ethanol via a sequence of steps 
known as the Guerbet reaction. In this cascade process, ethanol first 
experienced dehydrogenation to form acetaldehyde, followed by aldol 
condensation of acetaldehyde and subsequently hydrogenation of cro­
tonaldehyde to produce n-butanol, which might undergo further 
coupling with ethanol or n-butanol to give higher carbon number al­
cohols. Since introduction of copper into OMA and OM-La2.6Al100 
dramatically improved the ethanol conversion and yield to higher al­
cohols, the highly dispersed CuAl2O4 on the OM-CuxLa2.6Al100 catalysts 
was main active phase for the ethanol dehydrogenation. Mendes et al. 
[70] found that Rh-Cu/Al2O3 catalyst with Cu2+
Octa species showed high 
activity in n-hexanol dehydrogenation. The Cu0.5Zn0.5Fe2O4 catalyst 
prepared by microwave combustion showed isopropanol conversion of 
59.8% in isopropanol dehydrogenation at 523 K due to existence of a 
large number of octahedrally coordinated Cu2+ and Fe3+ active sites 
[71]. Riittonen et al. [29] found that Cu/γ-Al2O3 catalyst with low
