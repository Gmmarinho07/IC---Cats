========== ABSTRACT ==========

ABSTRACT: Ethanol, mainly produced from the yeast fermentation
of biomass, is one of the most important renewable building blocks for
synthesizing value-added chemicals. Different pathways of ethanol
conversion, including dehydrogenation, C−C bond coupling, aroma-
tization, hydrogen transfer, dehydration, etc., can be achieved over a
variety of catalysts. In this Perspective, we focus on the catalytic one-pot
ethanol conversion processes for producing oxygen-containing value-
added chemicals. The details include the catalyst design strategy,
selectivity manipulation, and mechanism investigation. We hope this
Perspective will inspire future catalyst design for reactions by tailoring
the C−H bond, C−O bond, or O−H bond and shed light on industry
applications with carbon neutrality.
KEYWORDS: ethanol, dehydrogenation, C−C coupling, aromatization, catalyst design

3.3. Other Supported Metal Catalysts. Li et al. reported
that Cu−CeO2/AC exhibited high performance as a catalyst
for the conversion of ethanol to n-butanol for the first time.49
The yield of n-butanol could reach nearly 20% under mild

3. C−C COUPLING TO HIGHER ALCOHOLS (C4−C12
ALCOHOLS)
The higher alcohols can be produced from ethanol through
direct coupling or the Guerbet pathway, involving dehydrogen-
ation, C−C coupling, and hydrogenation steps. Both acid and
base sites are necessary to achieve high C−C coupling rates.
Hydroxyapatite (HAP) with an acid−base pair has been
proven to be suitable for this reaction, and mixed oxides
derived from hydrotalcites (HT) are also efficient with a
tunable structure of acid−base sites. Thus, we will focus on the
introduction of HAP- and HT-derived catalysts in this section.

phosphate compound with a Ca/P molar ratio ranging from

lattice. Moreover, Ca2+ ions on the HAP surface are easily

hexagonal unit cell, thus presenting broad opportunities for the
design of multifunctional catalysts with atomically dispersed
metal sites. Meanwhile, the anions in HAP can also be replaced
to form catalysts with different acid−base densities and
strength.

to n-butanol and higher aliphatic alcohols (C6−12−OH) in the

active nonstoichiometric HAP catalyst.29 The highest n-

that both the catalytic activity and higher alcohol selectivity
increased over HAP with a larger Ca/P molar ratio due to the
higher density of basic sites.30 Ogo et al. systematically

the highest 1-butanol selectivity among the three metal
phosphates due to its intermediate-strength Lewis acidity.
Brasil et al. synthesized HAP by different preparation methods
(ultrasound, microwave, and autoclave) to change the acidic/
basic sites and investigated its activity for ethanol conversion.34
The highest selectivity for butanol occurred on HAP treated
with a microwave for 30 min due to its greater number of basic
sites and better balance of acidic and basic sites. Additionally,
they first reported the DFT calculations for key elementary
steps for ethanol conversion on HAP.
