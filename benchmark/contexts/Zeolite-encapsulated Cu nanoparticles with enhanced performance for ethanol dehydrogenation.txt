========== EXPERIMENTAL ==========

Experimental
2.1. Preparation of materials
Synthesis of Cu@S-1: Tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS, 20.00 g,
99.7 %, Tianjin Kemiou) and tetrapropylammonium hydroxide
(TPAOH, 11.40 g, 25 %, Tianjin Guangfu) were ﬁrst added into
deionized H2O (27 mL) and stirred to form a clear solution. The
obtained solution was then heated in a water bath and kept at
333 K for 1.5 h under stirring to form the starting gel of S-1. Then,
Cu(NO3)23H2O (0.43 g, 99 %, Tianjin Damao) was dissolved into
deionized H2O (10 mL) and mixed with tetraethylenepentamine
(TEPA, 0.37 g, 95 %, TCI Shanghai), with the appearance of a dark
blue solution. The obtained Cu-TEPA solution was added into the
starting gel of S-1 and subjected at 443 K for 48 h under hydrother-
mal conditions in a Teﬂon-lined stainless-steel autoclave. The solid
powder was obtained after separation by centrifugation, washing
with deionized H2O and drying at 373 K overnight. The obtained
solid catalyst was calcined at 823 K for 6 h in air and subsequently
cooled rapidly to room temperature. Cu@S-1 was then obtained
and used after reduction at 673 K for 2 h under a ﬂow of 5 vol%
H2/N2.
Synthesis of Cu/S-1: TEOS (20.00 g) and TPAOH (11.40 g, 25 %)
were ﬁrst added into deionized H2O (27 mL) and stirred to form a
clear solution. The obtained solution was then heated in a water
bath and kept at 333 K for 1.5 h under stirring to form the starting
gel of S-1 and then subjected at 443 K for 48 h under hydrothermal
conditions in a Teﬂon-lined stainless-steel autoclave. The solid
powder was obtained after separation by centrifugation, washing
with deionized H2O and drying at 373 K overnight. The obtained
solid catalyst was calcined at 823 K for 6 h in air to obtain S-1.
For loading Cu species, Cu(NO3)23H2O (0.076 g) was dissolved in
deionized H2O (1.45 mL), and then the aqueous solution was added
into the S-1 (1.00 g) dropwise with stirring. After drying at room
temperature for 12 h, the mixture was transferred to an oven at

353 K overnight, followed by calcination at 673 K for 4 h. Cu/
SiO2 was then obtained and used after reduction at 673 K for 2 h
under a ﬂow of 5 vol% H2/N2.
2.2. Characterizations
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was carried out using the PW3040/60
X’Pert Pro powder X-ray diffractometer from PANAnalytical, the
Netherlands. The diffraction source was Cu Ka ray (k = 1.5432 Å),
Ni ﬁltered, LynxExe array detector, tube voltage and tube current
were 40 KV and 40 mA, respectively. The scanning step is 0.02.
The crystalline phase was identiﬁed with reference to the ICDD
PDF-2 database. The actual Cu loading of catalysts was determined
by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-
OES) on an IRIS Intrepid II XSP instrument (Thermo Electron Corpo-
ration) with a sensitivity line of 349.8 nm and a detection range of
1–10 ppm. N2 physisorption isotherms were obtained at 77 K using
a Micromeritics ASAP 2460 instrument, all samples were outgassed
at 473 K for 12 h prior to the physisorption measurements. Scan-
ning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) images were
obtained with a JEOL JEM-2100F microscope operated at 200 kV.
Energy dispersive X-ray spectra (EDX-mapping) were collected
with a JEOL Oxford X-MaxN 80 T silicon drift detector, by rastering
the beam over the target zone

Results and discussion
3.1. Physicochemical properties of different catalysts
Scheme 1 illustrates the in-situ synthesis strategy of Cu@S-1. In
detail, Cu2+ cations ﬁrst coordinate with polyethylenepolyamine
(TEPA) to avoid precipitation under alkaline hydrothermal condi-
tions during the zeolite S-1 synthesis. The as-obtained Cu-TEPA
complexes could be captured during the zeolite assembly process
and thus encapsulated within the cavities of the zeolite via the
interaction between the metal complex and the zeolite [33,36].
After a subsequent calcination at 550 C for 6 h in air and a follow-
ing reduction at 400 C for 2 h under 5 % H2/N2 atmosphere, well-
dispersed Cu nanoparticles within the cavities of S-1 could be
obtained, denoted as Cu@S-1. Cu/S-1 and Cu/SiO2 as benchmark
catalysts were also synthesized via a wet impregnation method.
X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of Cu/S-1 and Cu@S-1 show the
characteristic diffraction peaks of an MFI topological structure
(Fig. S1a), indicating that the addition of Cu species has limited
impact on the formation of the S-1 zeolite. Cu/SiO2 also retain
the patterns of the supports after Cu loading (Fig. S1b). The absence
of characteristic peaks of Cu species in all of the three Cu-
containing catalysts is possibly due to the low Cu loading of
2.00 wt%, as determined by inductively coupled plasma atomic
emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES). Table 1 lists the physicochemical
properties of different samples. Compared with the parent S-1,

Temperature-programmed reduction of hydrogen (H2-TPR)
measurements were also carried out on Cu/SiO2, Cu/S-1 and
Cu@S-1 catalysts. As shown in Fig. 5d, the reduction temperature
of Cu species in Cu@S-1 (536 K) are higher than that of Cu/SiO2
(461 K) and Cu/S-1 (502 K). Li and co-workers also reported that
the reduction peaks of encapsulated Pd nanoparticles in Pd@S-1
were observed in the higher temperature range (443–523 K) than
those of nonencapsulated Pd species in Pd/S-1 (423–503 K) [50].
Therefore, Cu species in Cu@S-1 are more difﬁcult to be reduced
than the counterparts in Cu/SiO2 and Cu/S-1 during H2 treatment.
This further conﬁrms the existence of an enhance interaction
between the encapsulated Cu species and the silanol groups from
S-1, which might stabilize Cu+ species and thus result into the pre-
dominant amount of Cu+ species in Cu@S-1.
